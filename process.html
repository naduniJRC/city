Edition 01
Completed
Sri Lanka · June–July 2024 · University of Moratuwa

Colombo

Adaptive Reuse in Pettah

The context of investigation in Colombo was Pettah, a vibrant historic precinct originally established as a Portuguese port city in the 1500s. The workshop focused on analyzing social, economic, and environmental challenges in the bustling urban landscape where participants worked out adaptive reuse strategies for the Old Town Hall and market buildings. Through site investigations, scaled drawings, and physical models, students developed comprehensive architectural solutions for repurposing these heritage structures, engaging with local stakeholders to ensure contextually sensitive proposals.

Adaptive Reuse Heritage Tropical Urbanism
Spain · February 2025 · Universitat La Salle – Ramon Llull

Barcelona

Radical Landscapes — Superblocks as Infrastructure

The workshop in Barcelona explored the city's innovative superblock (superilla) model as a framework for addressing climate emergency and urban sustainability. Working within a defined superblock unit in central Barcelona, teams developed proposals for permanent urban climate islands designed to mitigate rising temperatures through shaded structures, tree planting, permeable soft surfaces, and thermal comfort strategies. Students investigated how to transform streets designed for cars into pedestrian-centred environments that promote street life, local commerce, and environmental innovation.

Superblock Climate Public Space

China · July 2025 · Xian Jiaotong-Liverpool University

Shanghai

Elevated Infrastructures and Urban Configurations

The Shanghai workshop examined the city's elevated expressway system at the intersection of North-South Elevated Road and Yan'an Road. Shanghai is a city of extreme density in terms of built fabric, population, and traffic. Elevated infrastructures often act as harsh physical barriers, dramatically dividing parts of the city. Re-thinking these spaces as potential urban realms addressing public space and community facilities can help redress these conditions. Through different present and future scenarios, students investigated and proposed architectural solutions that improve circulation, liveability, and the use of public spaces.

Infrastructure High Density Public Realm
Japan · July 2026 · University of Tsukuba

Tokyo

Architecture Linking City, Suburbs and Ecosystems

The Tokyo workshop explored the relationship between Tokyo's ultra-modern patchwork metropolis and its surrounding suburban ecosystems. Participants studied Tokyo's layered urban history, from Edo-period waterways to post-war reconstruction and contemporary challenges of social isolation. Working with the Mokuchin housing typology, students conducted field surveys and developed proposals for spatial interventions. A hands-on component involved learning traditional bamboo craftsmanship in Hojo, Tsukuba — harvesting local bamboo, thinning groves for conservation, and constructing full-scale temporary pavilions exhibited in Tokyo.

Bamboo Suburb & Ecology Megalopolis

Chile · TBD · Universidad San Sebastián

Patagonia

Green Infrastructure and Territorial Systems

The Patagonia workshop shifts focus from conventional urban questions to a territorial scale where infrastructure, tourism, conservation, energy production, and human settlements generate dispersed, environmentally sensitive urbanization. Using the Llanquihue-Maullín watershed as the primary spatial structure, participants investigate how water structures territory, how waterfronts function as transition landscapes between city and nature, and how architecture can foster resilient relationships between ecological processes, infrastructure, and public life.

Green Infrastructure Territory Watershed
